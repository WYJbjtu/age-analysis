L492:     Reliability of the estimates
L493: 
L494:        The estimates presented in this release are based on sample surveys,
L495:     administrative data, and modeling and, thus, are subject to sampling and
L496:     other types of errors. Sampling error is a measure of sampling variability--
L497:     that is, variation that occurs by chance because a sample rather than the
L498:     entire population is surveyed. Survey data also are subject to nonsampling
L499:     errors, such as those which can be introduced into the data collection and
L500:     processing operations. Estimates not directly derived from sample surveys
L501:     are subject to additional errors resulting from the specific estimation
L502:     processes used. In table 1, level estimates for states may not sum to level
L503:     estimates for regions and divisions because of rounding. Unemployment rates
L504:     and employment-population ratios are computed from unrounded levels and thus
L505:     may differ slightly from rates and ratios computed using the rounded level
L506:     estimates displayed in table 1.
L507: 
L508:        Use of error measures. In 2005, the LAUS program introduced several
L509:     improvements to its methodology. Among these was the development of model-
L510:     based error measures for the monthly estimates and the estimates of over-
L511:     the-month changes. Annual average model-based error measures became avail-
L512:     able for the first time after 2006. The introductory section of this release
L513:     preserves the long-time practice of highlighting the direction of the move-
L514:     ments in regional and state unemployment rates and employment-population
L515:     ratios regardless of their statistical significance. The remainder of the
L516:     analysis in the release--other than historical highs and lows--takes statis-
L517:     tical significance into consideration. Model-based error measures are avail-
L518:     able online at www.bls.gov/lau/lastderr.htm. BLS uses 90-percent confidence
L519:     levels in determining whether changes in LAUS unemployment rates or employment-
L520:     population ratios are statistically significant. The average magnitude of the
L521:     over-the-year change in an annual state unemployment rate that is required in
L522:     order to be statistically significant at the 90-percent confidence level is
L523:     about 0.5 percentage point. The average magnitude of the over-the-year change
L524:     in an annual state employment-population ratio that is required in order to
L525:     be statistically significant at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.6
L526:     percentage point. More details can be found on the Web site. Measures of non-
L527:     sampling error are not available, but additional information on the subject is
L528:     provided in Employment & Earnings Online, available on the BLS Web site at
L529:     www.bls.gov/opub/ee/home.htm.
