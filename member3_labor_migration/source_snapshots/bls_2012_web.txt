L470:        This release presents labor force and unemployment data for census regions
L471:     and divisions and states from the Local Area Unemployment Statistics (LAUS)
L472:     program. The LAUS program is a federal-state cooperative endeavor.
L473: 
L474:     Concepts
L475: 
L476:        Definitions. The labor force and unemployment data are based on the same
L477:     concepts and definitions as those used for the official national estimates
L478:     obtained from the Current Population Survey (CPS), a sample survey of house-
L479:     holds that is conducted for the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) by the U.S.
L480:     Census Bureau. The LAUS program measures employment and unemployment on a
L481:     place-of-residence basis. The universe for each is the civilian noninstitu-
L482:     tional population 16 years of age and over. Employed persons are those who did
L483:     any work at all for pay or profit in the reference week (the week including
L484:     the 12th of the month) or worked 15 hours or more without pay in a family
L485:     business or farm, plus those not working who had a job from which they were
L486:     temporarily absent, whether or not paid, for such reasons as labor-manage-
L487:     ment dispute, illness, or vacation. Unemployed persons are those who were
L488:     not employed during the reference week (based on the definition above), had
L489:     actively looked for a job sometime in the 4-week period ending with the
L490:     reference week, and were currently available for work; persons on layoff
L491:     expecting recall need not be looking for work to be counted as unemployed.
L492:     The labor force is the sum of employed and unemployed persons. The unemploy-
L493:     ment rate is the number of unemployed expressed as a percent of the labor
L494:     force. The employment-population ratio is the proportion of the civilian
L495:     noninstitutional population 16 years of age and over that is employed.
L496: 
L497:        Method of estimation. Estimates for 48 of the 50 states, the District of
L498:     Columbia, the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale metropolitan division, New York
L499:     City, and the balances of California and New York State are produced using
L500:     estimating equations based on regression techniques. This method, which under-
L501:     went substantial enhancement at the beginning of 2005, utilizes data from
L502:     several sources, including the CPS, the Current Employment Statistics (CES)
L503:     survey of nonfarm payroll employment, and state unemployment insurance (UI)
L504:     programs. Estimates for the State of California are derived by summing the
L505:     estimates for the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale metropolitan division and
L506:     the balance of California. Similarly, estimates for New York State are derived
L507:     by summing the estimates for New York City and the balance of New York State.
L508:     Estimates for all nine census divisions are based on a similar regression
L509:     approach that does not incorporate CES or UI data. Estimates for census
L510:     regions are obtained by summing the model-based estimates for the component
L511:     divisions and then calculating the unemployment rate. Each month, census
L512:     division estimates are controlled to national totals; state estimates are
L513:     then controlled to their respective division totals. Estimates for Puerto Rico
L514:     are derived from a monthly household survey similar to the CPS. A detailed
L515:     description of the estimation procedures is available from BLS upon request.
L516:        Annual revisions. Labor force and unemployment data for prior years reflect
L517:     adjustments made at the end of each year. The adjusted estimates incorporate
L518:     updated population data from the U.S. Census Bureau, any revisions in the
L519:     other data sources, and model reestimation. The population data (except for
L520:     Puerto Rico) reflect, for the first time, the results of the 2010 Census. In
L521:     most years, historical data for the most recent 5 years (both seasonally
L522:     adjusted and not seasonally adjusted) are revised near the beginning of each
L523:     calendar year, prior to the release of January estimates. Though the labor
L524:     force estimates are changed for 5 years, the population estimates are adjusted
L525:     back to the new decennial estimates base of April 2010.
L526: 
L527:     Reliability of the estimates
L528: 
L529:        The estimates presented in this release are based on sample surveys,
L530:     administrative data, and modeling and, thus, are subject to sampling and
L531:     other types of errors. Sampling error is a measure of sampling variability--
L532:     that is, variation that occurs by chance because a sample rather than the
L533:     entire population is surveyed. Survey data also are subject to nonsampling
L534:     errors, such as those which can be introduced into the data collection and
L535:     processing operations. Estimates not directly derived from sample surveys
L536:     are subject to additional errors resulting from the specific estimation
L537:     processes used. In table 1, level estimates for states may not sum to level
L538:     estimates for regions and divisions because of rounding. Unemployment rates
L539:     and employment-population ratios are computed from unrounded levels and thus
L540:     may differ slightly from rates and ratios computed using the rounded level
L541:     estimates displayed in table 1.
L542: 
L543:        Use of error measures. In 2005, the LAUS program introduced several
L544:     improvements to its methodology. Among these was the development of model-
L545:     based error measures for the monthly estimates and the estimates of over-
L546:     the-month changes. Annual average model-based error measures became avail-
L547:     able for the first time after 2006. The introductory section of this release
L548:     preserves the long-time practice of highlighting the direction of the move-
L549:     ments in regional and state unemployment rates and employment-population
L550:     ratios regardless of their statistical significance. The remainder of the
L551:     analysis in the release--other than historical highs and lows--takes statis-
L552:     tical significance into consideration. Model-based error measures are avail-
L553:     able online at www.bls.gov/lau/lastderr.htm. BLS uses 90-percent confidence
L554:     levels in determining whether changes in LAUS unemployment rates or employment-
L555:     population ratios are statistically significant. The average magnitude of the
L556:     over-the-year change in an annual state unemployment rate that is required in
L557:     order to be statistically significant at the 90-percent confidence level is
L558:     about 0.5 percentage point. The average magnitude of the over-the-year change
L559:     in an annual state employment-population ratio that is required in order to
L560:     be statistically significant at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.6
L561:     percentage point. Measures of nonsampling error are not available.
