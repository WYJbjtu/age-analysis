L498:        Annual revisions. Labor force and unemployment data for prior years reflect
L499:     adjustments made at the end of each year. The adjusted estimates incorporate
L500:     updated population data from the U.S. Census Bureau, any revisions in the
L501:     other data sources, and model reestimation. The population data (except for
L502:     Puerto Rico) reflect, for the first time, the results of the 2010 Census. In
L503:     most years, historical data for the most recent 5 years (both seasonally
L504:     adjusted and not seasonally adjusted) are revised near the beginning of each
L505:     calendar year, prior to the release of January estimates. Though the labor
L506:     force estimates are changed for 5 years, the population estimates are adjusted
L507:     back to the new decennial estimates base of April 2010.

L509:     Reliability of the estimates
L510: 
L511:        The estimates presented in this release are based on sample surveys,
L512:     administrative data, and modeling and, thus, are subject to sampling and
L513:     other types of errors. Sampling error is a measure of sampling variability--
L514:     that is, variation that occurs by chance because a sample rather than the
L515:     entire population is surveyed. Survey data also are subject to nonsampling
L516:     errors, such as those which can be introduced into the data collection and
L517:     processing operations. Estimates not directly derived from sample surveys
L518:     are subject to additional errors resulting from the specific estimation
L519:     processes used. In table 1, level estimates for states may not sum to level
L520:     estimates for regions and divisions because of rounding. Unemployment rates
L521:     and employment-population ratios are computed from unrounded levels and thus
L522:     may differ slightly from rates and ratios computed using the rounded level
L523:     estimates displayed in table 1.
L524: 
L525:        Use of error measures. In 2005, the LAUS program introduced several
L526:     improvements to its methodology. Among these was the development of model-
L527:     based error measures for the monthly estimates and the estimates of over-
L528:     the-month changes. Annual average model-based error measures became avail-
L529:     able for the first time after 2006. The introductory section of this release
L530:     preserves the long-time practice of highlighting the direction of the move-
L531:     ments in regional and state unemployment rates and employment-population
L532:     ratios regardless of their statistical significance. The remainder of the
L533:     analysis in the release--other than historical highs and lows--takes statis-
L534:     tical significance into consideration. Model-based error measures are avail-
L535:     able online at www.bls.gov/lau/lastderr.htm. BLS uses 90-percent confidence
L536:     levels in determining whether changes in LAUS unemployment rates or employment-
L537:     population ratios are statistically significant. The average magnitude of the
L538:     over-the-year change in an annual state unemployment rate that is required in
L539:     order to be statistically significant at the 90-percent confidence level is
L540:     about 0.5 percentage point. The average magnitude of the over-the-year change
L541:     in an annual state employment-population ratio that is required in order to
L542:     be statistically significant at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.6
L543:     percentage point. Measures of nonsampling error are not available.
