L476:     Technical Note
L477: 
L478:     This release presents labor force and unemployment data for census regions
L479:     and divisions and states from the Local Area Unemployment Statistics (LAUS)
L480:     program. The LAUS program is a federal-state cooperative endeavor.
L481: 
L482:     Concepts
L483: 
L484:     Definitions. The labor force and unemployment data are based on the same
L485:     concepts and definitions as those used for the official national estimates
L486:     obtained from the Current Population Survey (CPS), a sample survey of
L487:     households that is conducted for the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) by
L488:     the U.S. Census Bureau. The LAUS program measures employment and unemployment
L489:     on a place-of-residence basis. The universe for each is the civilian
L490:     noninstitutional population 16 years of age and older. Employed persons are
L491:     those who did any work at all for pay or profit in the reference week (the
L492:     week including the 12th of the month) or worked 15 hours or more without
L493:     pay in a family business or farm, plus those not working who had a job from
L494:     which they were temporarily absent, whether or not paid, for such reasons
L495:     as labor management dispute, illness, or vacation. Unemployed persons are
L496:     those who were not employed during the reference week (based on the
L497:     definition above), had actively looked for a job sometime in the 4-week
L498:     period ending with the reference week, and were currently available for
L499:     work; persons on layoff expecting recall need not be looking for work to
L500:     be counted as unemployed. The labor force is the sum of employed and
L501:     unemployed persons. The unemployment rate is the number of unemployed
L502:     expressed as a percent of the labor force. The employment-population ratio
L503:     is the proportion of the civilian noninstitutional population 16 years of
L504:     age and older that is employed.
L505: 
L506:     Method of estimation. Estimates for 48 of the 50 states, the District of
L507:     Columbia, the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale metropolitan division, New
L508:     York City, and the balances of California and New York State are produced
L509:     using estimating equations based on regression techniques. This method
L510:     utilizes data from several sources, including the CPS, the Current
L511:     Employment Statistics (CES) survey of nonfarm payroll employment, and
L512:     state unemployment insurance (UI) programs. Estimates for the State of
L513:     California are derived by summing the estimates for the Los Angeles-Long
L514:     Beach-Glendale metropolitan division and the balance of California.
L515:     Similarly, estimates for New York State are derived by summing the
L516:     estimates for New York City and the balance of New York State. Estimates
L517:     for all nine census divisions are based on a similar regression approach
L518:     that does not incorporate CES or UI data. Estimates for census regions
L519:     are obtained by summing the model-based estimates for the component
L520:     divisions and then calculating the unemployment rate. Each month, census
L521:     division estimates are controlled to national totals; state estimates
L522:     are then controlled to their respective division totals. A detailed
L523:     description of the estimation procedures is available from BLS upon
L524:     request.
L525: 
L526:     Annual revisions. Labor force and unemployment data for prior years
L527:     reflect adjustments made at the beginning of each year. The adjusted
L528:     estimates incorporate updated population controls from the U.S. Census
L529:     Bureau, any revisions in the other data sources, and model re-estimation.
L530:     The population controls reflect extrapolation from the 2010 Census. In
L531:     most years, historical data for the most recent 5 years (both seasonally
L532:     adjusted and not seasonally adjusted) are revised near the beginning of
L533:     each calendar year, prior to the release of January estimates. Though
L534:     the labor force estimates typically are updated for 5 years, the
L535:     population estimates are revised back to the decennial estimates base
L536:     (April 2010).
L537: 
L538:     Reliability of the estimates
L539: 
L540:     The estimates presented in this release are based on sample surveys,
L541:     administrative data, and modeling and, thus, are subject to sampling and
L542:     other types of errors. Sampling error is a measure of sampling variability--
L543:     that is, variation that occurs by chance because a sample rather than
L544:     the entire population is surveyed. Survey data also are subject to
L545:     nonsampling errors, such as those which can be introduced into the data
L546:     collection and processing operations. Estimates not directly derived
L547:     from sample surveys are subject to additional errors resulting from the
L548:     specific estimation processes used. In table 1, level estimates for
L549:     states may not sum to level estimates for regions and divisions because
L550:     of rounding. Unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are
L551:     computed from unrounded levels and, thus, may differ slightly from rates
L552:     and ratios computed using the rounded level estimates displayed in table 1.
L553:     Use of error measures. The introductory section of this release preserves
L554:     the long-time practice of highlighting the direction of the movements in
L555:     regional and state unemployment rates and employment-population ratios
L556:     regardless of their statistical significance. The remainder of the
L557:     analysis in the release--other than historical highs and lows--takes
L558:     statistical significance into consideration. Model-based error measures
L559:     are available online at www.bls.gov/lau/lastderr.htm. BLS uses 90-percent
L560:     confidence levels in determining whether changes in LAUS unemployment
L561:     rates and employment-population ratios are statistically significant. The
L562:     average magnitude of the over-the-year change in an annual state
L563:     unemployment rate that is required in order to be statistically significant
L564:     at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.4 percentage point. The
L565:     average magnitude of the over-the-year change in an annual state employment-
L566:     population ratio that is required in order to be statistically significant
L567:     at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.6 percentage point. Measures
L568:     of nonsampling error are not available.
