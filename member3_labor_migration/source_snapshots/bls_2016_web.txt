L503:     The estimates presented in this release are based on sample surveys, administrative data,
L504:     and modeling and, thus, are subject to sampling and other types of errors. Sampling error
L505:     is a measure of sampling variability�that is, variation that occurs by chance because a
L506:     sample rather than the entire population is surveyed. Survey data also are subject to
L507:     nonsampling errors, such as those which can be introduced into the data collection and
L508:     processing operations. Estimates not directly derived from sample surveys are subject to
L509:     additional errors resulting from the specific estimation processes used. In table 1, level
L510:     estimates for states may not sum to level estimates for regions and divisions because of
L511:     rounding. Unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are computed from unrounded
L512:     levels and, thus, may differ slightly from rates and ratios computed using the rounded
L513:     level estimates displayed in table 1.
L514: 
L515:     Use of error measures. The introductory section of this release preserves the long-time
L516:     practice of highlighting the direction of the movements in regional and state unemployment
L517:     rates and employment-population ratios regardless of their statistical significance. The
L518:     remainder of the analysis in the release�other than historical highs and lows�takes
L519:     statistical significance into consideration. Model-based error measures are available
L520:     online at www.bls.gov/lau/lastderr.htm. BLS uses 90-percent confidence levels in
L521:     determining whether changes in LAUS unemployment rates and employment-population ratios
L522:     are statistically significant. The average magnitude of the over-the-year change in an
L523:     annual state unemployment rate that is required in order to be statistically significant
L524:     at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.4 percentage point. The average magnitude
L525:     of the over-the-year change in an annual state employment-population ratio that is
L526:     required in order to be statistically significant at the 90-percent confidence level is
L527:     about 0.6 percentage point. Measures of nonsampling error are not available.
