L483:     Method of estimation. Estimates for 48 of the 50 states, the District of
L484:     Columbia, the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale metropolitan division, New
L485:     York City, and the balances of California and New York State are produced
L486:     using estimating equations based on regression techniques. This method
L487:     utilizes data from several sources, including the CPS, the Current
L488:     Employment Statistics (CES) survey of nonfarm payroll employment, and
L489:     state unemployment insurance (UI) programs. Estimates for the State of
L490:     California are derived by summing the estimates for the Los Angeles-Long
L491:     Beach-Glendale metropolitan division and the balance of California.
L492:     Similarly, estimates for New York State are derived by summing the
L493:     estimates for New York City and the balance of New York State. Estimates
L494:     for all nine census divisions are based on a similar regression approach
L495:     that does not incorporate CES or UI data. Estimates for census regions
L496:     are obtained by summing the model-based estimates for the component
L497:     divisions and then calculating the unemployment rate. Each month, census
L498:     division estimates are controlled to national totals; state estimates
L499:     are then controlled to their respective division totals. Estimates for
L500:     Puerto Rico are derived from a monthly household survey similar to the
L501:     CPS. A detailed description of the estimation procedures is available
L502:     from BLS upon request.
L503: 
L504:     Annual revisions. Labor force and unemployment data for prior years
L505:     reflect adjustments made at the beginning of each year. The adjusted
L506:     estimates incorporate updated population controls from the U.S. Census
L507:     Bureau, any revisions in the other data sources, and model re-estimation.
L508:     The population controls reflect extrapolation from the 2010 Census. In
L509:     most years, historical data for the most recent 5 years (both seasonally
L510:     adjusted and not seasonally adjusted) are revised near the beginning of
L511:     each calendar year, prior to the release of January estimates. Though
L512:     the labor force estimates typically are updated for 5 years, the
L513:     population estimates are revised back to the decennial estimates base
L514:     (April 2010).
L515: 
L516:     Reliability of the estimates
L517:     The estimates presented in this release are based on sample surveys,
L518:     administrative data, and modeling and, thus, are subject to sampling and
L519:     other types of errors. Sampling error is a measure of sampling variability--
L520:     that is, variation that occurs by chance because a sample rather than
L521:     the entire population is surveyed. Survey data also are subject to
L522:     nonsampling errors, such as those which can be introduced into the data
L523:     collection and processing operations. Estimates not directly derived
L524:     from sample surveys are subject to additional errors resulting from the
L525:     specific estimation processes used. In table 1, level estimates for
L526:     states may not sum to level estimates for regions and divisions because
L527:     of rounding. Unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are
L528:     computed from unrounded levels and, thus, may differ slightly from rates
L529:     and ratios computed using the rounded level estimates displayed in table 1.
L530: 
L531:     Use of error measures. Changes in unemployment rates and employment-population
L532:     ratios are cited in the analysis of this release only if they have been determined
L533:     to be statistically significant. Furthermore, unemployment rates and
L534:     employment-population ratios for the latest year generally are cited only
L535:     if they have been determined to be significantly different from the corresponding
L536:     U.S. measure. The underlying model-based error measures are available online at
L537:     www.bls.gov/lau/lastderr.htm. BLS uses 90-percent confidence levels in determining
L538:     whether changes in LAUS unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are
L539:     statistically significant. The average magnitude of the over-the-year change in
L540:     an annual state unemployment rate that is required in order to be statistically
L541:     significant at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.4 percentage point. The
L542:     average magnitude of the over-the-year change in an annual state employment-
L543:     population ratio that is required in order to be statistically significant
L544:     at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.6 percentage point. Measures
L545:     of nonsampling error are not available.
