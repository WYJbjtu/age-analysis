L456:     Technical Note
L457: 
L458:     This release presents labor force and unemployment data for census regions
L459:     and divisions and states from the Local Area Unemployment Statistics (LAUS)
L460:     program. The LAUS program is a federal-state cooperative endeavor.
L461: 
L462:     Concepts
L463: 
L464:     Definitions. The labor force and unemployment data are based on the same
L465:     concepts and definitions as those used for the official national estimates
L466:     obtained from the Current Population Survey (CPS), a sample survey of
L467:     households that is conducted for the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) by
L468:     the U.S. Census Bureau. The LAUS program measures employed and unemployed
L469:     persons on a place-of-residence basis. The universe for each is the civilian
L470:     noninstitutional population 16 years of age and older. Employed persons are
L471:     those who did any work at all for pay or profit in the reference week (the
L472:     week including the 12th of the month) or worked 15 hours or more without
L473:     pay in a family business or farm, plus those not working who had a job from
L474:     which they were temporarily absent, whether or not paid, for such reasons
L475:     as labor management dispute, illness, or vacation. Unemployed persons are
L476:     those who were not employed during the reference week (based on the
L477:     definition above), had actively looked for a job sometime in the 4-week
L478:     period ending with the reference week, and were currently available for
L479:     work; persons on layoff expecting recall need not be looking for work to
L480:     be counted as unemployed. The labor force is the sum of employed and
L481:     unemployed persons. The unemployment rate is the number of unemployed
L482:     expressed as a percent of the labor force. The employment-population ratio
L483:     is the proportion of the civilian noninstitutional population 16 years of
L484:     age and older that is employed.
L485: 
L486:     Method of estimation. Estimates for 48 of the 50 states, the District of
L487:     Columbia, the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale metropolitan division, New
L488:     York City, and the balances of California and New York State are produced
L489:     using estimating equations based on regression techniques. This method
L490:     utilizes data from several sources, including the CPS, the Current
L491:     Employment Statistics (CES) survey of nonfarm payroll employment, and
L492:     state unemployment insurance (UI) programs. Estimates for the State of
L493:     California are derived by summing the estimates for the Los Angeles-Long
L494:     Beach-Glendale metropolitan division and the balance of California.
L495:     Similarly, estimates for New York State are derived by summing the
L496:     estimates for New York City and the balance of New York State. Estimates
L497:     for all nine census divisions are based on a similar regression approach
L498:     that does not incorporate CES or UI data. Estimates for census regions
L499:     are obtained by summing the model-based estimates for the component
L500:     divisions and then calculating the unemployment rate. Each month, census
L501:     division estimates are controlled to national totals; state estimates
L502:     are then controlled to their respective division totals. Estimates for
L503:     Puerto Rico are derived from a monthly household survey similar to the
L504:     CPS. A detailed description of the estimation procedures is available
L505:     from BLS upon request.
L506: 
L507:     Annual revisions. Labor force and unemployment data for prior years
L508:     reflect adjustments made at the beginning of each year. The adjusted
L509:     estimates incorporate updated population controls from the U.S. Census
L510:     Bureau, any revisions in the other data sources, and model re-estimation.
L511:     The population controls reflect extrapolation from the 2010 Census. In
L512:     most years, historical data for the most recent 5 years (both seasonally
L513:     adjusted and not seasonally adjusted) are revised near the beginning of
L514:     each calendar year, prior to the release of January estimates. Though
L515:     the labor force estimates typically are updated for 5 years, the
L516:     population estimates are revised back to the decennial estimates base
L517:     (April 2010).
L518: 
L519:     Reliability of the estimates
L520:     The estimates presented in this release are based on sample surveys,
L521:     administrative data, and modeling and, thus, are subject to sampling and
L522:     other types of errors. Sampling error is a measure of sampling variability--
L523:     that is, variation that occurs by chance because a sample rather than
L524:     the entire population is surveyed. Survey data also are subject to
L525:     nonsampling errors, such as those which can be introduced into the data
L526:     collection and processing operations. Estimates not directly derived
L527:     from sample surveys are subject to additional errors resulting from the
L528:     specific estimation processes used. In table 1, level estimates for
L529:     states may not sum to level estimates for regions and divisions because
L530:     of rounding. Unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are
L531:     computed from unrounded levels and, thus, may differ slightly from rates
L532:     and ratios computed using the rounded level estimates displayed in table 1.
L533: 
L534:     Use of error measures. Changes in unemployment rates and employment-population
L535:     ratios are cited in the analysis of this release only if they have been determined
L536:     to be statistically significant. Furthermore, unemployment rates and
L537:     employment-population ratios for the latest year generally are cited only
L538:     if they have been determined to be significantly different from the corresponding
L539:     U.S. measure. The underlying model-based error measures are available online at
L540:     www.bls.gov/lau/lastderr.htm. BLS uses 90-percent confidence levels in determining
L541:     whether changes in LAUS unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are
L542:     statistically significant. The average magnitude of the over-the-year change in
L543:     an annual state unemployment rate that is required in order to be statistically
L544:     significant at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.4 percentage point. The
L545:     average magnitude of the over-the-year change in an annual state employment-
L546:     population ratio that is required in order to be statistically significant
L547:     at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.5 percentage point. Measures
L548:     of nonsampling error are not available.
