L624:     Method of estimation. Estimates for 48 of the 50 states, the District of
L627:     using estimating equations based on regression techniques. This method
L628:     utilizes data from several sources, including the CPS, the Current
L629:     Employment Statistics (CES) survey of nonfarm payroll employment, and
L630:     state unemployment insurance (UI) programs. Estimates for the State of
L631:     California are derived by summing the estimates for the Los Angeles-Long

L633:     Similarly, estimates for New York State are derived by summing the
L634:     estimates for New York City and the balance of New York State. Estimates
L635:     for all nine census divisions are based on a similar regression approach
L636:     that does not incorporate CES or UI data. Estimates for census regions
L637:     are obtained by summing the model-based estimates for the component
L638:     divisions and then calculating the unemployment rate. Each month, census
L639:     division estimates are controlled to national totals; state estimates
L640:     are then controlled to their respective division totals. A detailed
L641:     description of the estimation procedures is available from BLS upon request.

L643:     Annual revisions. Labor force and unemployment data for prior years
L644:     reflect adjustments made at the beginning of each year. The adjusted
L645:     estimates incorporate updated population controls from the U.S. Census
L646:     Bureau, any revisions in the other data sources, and model re-estimation.
L647:     The population controls reflect extrapolation from the 2010 Census. In
L648:     most years, historical data for the most recent 5 years (both seasonally
L649:     adjusted and not seasonally adjusted) are revised near the beginning of
L650:     each calendar year, prior to the release of January estimates. Though
L651:     the labor force estimates typically are updated for 5 years, the
L652:     population estimates are revised back to the decennial estimates base
L653:     (April 2010). With the introduction of a new generation of times-series
L654:     models in 2021, historical data were re-estimated back to January 1976
L655:     for regions, divisions, states, the District of Columbia, the Los
L656:     Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale metropolitan division, New York City, and
L657:     the balances of California and New York states.

L659:     Reliability of the estimates
L660:     The estimates presented in this release are based on sample surveys,
L661:     administrative data, and modeling and, thus, are subject to sampling and
L662:     other types of errors. Sampling error is a measure of sampling variability--
L663:     that is, variation that occurs by chance because a sample rather than
L664:     the entire population is surveyed. Survey data also are subject to
L665:     nonsampling errors, such as those which can be introduced into the data
L666:     collection and processing operations. Estimates not directly derived
L667:     from sample surveys are subject to additional errors resulting from the
L668:     specific estimation processes used. In table 1, level estimates for
L669:     states may not sum to level estimates for regions and divisions because
L670:     of rounding. Unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are
L671:     computed from unrounded levels and, thus, may differ slightly from rates
L672:     and ratios computed using the rounded level estimates displayed in table 1.
L673: 
L674:     Use of error measures. Changes in unemployment rates and employment-population
L675:     ratios are cited in the analysis of this release only if they have been determined
L676:     to be statistically significant. Furthermore, unemployment rates and
L677:     employment-population ratios for the latest year generally are cited only
L678:     if they have been determined to be significantly different from the corresponding
L679:     U.S. measure. The underlying model-based error measures are available online at
L680:     www.bls.gov/lau/lastderr.htm. BLS uses 90-percent confidence levels in determining
L681:     whether changes in LAUS unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are
L682:     statistically significant. The average magnitude of the over-the-year change in
L683:     an annual state unemployment rate that is required in order to be statistically
L684:     significant at the 90-percent confidence level is about 1.0 percentage point. The
L685:     average magnitude of the over-the-year change in an annual state employment-
L686:     population ratio that is required in order to be statistically significant
L687:     at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.9 percentage point. Measures
L688:     of nonsampling error are not available.
