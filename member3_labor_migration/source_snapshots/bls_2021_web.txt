L521:     Technical Note
L522: 
L523:     This release presents labor force and unemployment data for census regions and divisions
L524:     and states from the Local Area Unemployment Statistics (LAUS) program. The LAUS program
L525:     is a federal-state cooperative endeavor.
L526: 
L527:     Concepts
L528:     Definitions. The labor force and unemployment data are based on the same concepts and
L529:     definitions as those used for the official national estimates obtained from the Current
L530:     Population Survey (CPS), a sample survey of households that is conducted for the Bureau
L531:     of Labor Statistics (BLS) by the U.S. Census Bureau. The LAUS program measures employed
L532:     and unemployed persons on a place-of-residence basis. The universe for each is the
L533:     civilian noninstitutional population 16 years of age and older. Employed persons are
L534:     those who did any work at all for pay or profit in the reference week (the week
L535:     including the 12th of the month) or worked 15 hours or more without pay in a family
L536:     business or farm, plus those not working who had a job from which they were temporarily
L537:     absent, whether or not paid, for such reasons as labor management dispute, illness, or
L538:     vacation. Unemployed persons are those who were not employed during the reference week
L539:     (based on the definition above), had actively looked for a job sometime in the 4-week
L540:     period ending with the reference week, and were currently available for work; persons
L541:     on layoff expecting recall need not be looking for work to be counted as unemployed. The
L542:     labor force is the sum of employed and unemployed persons. The unemployment rate is the
L543:     number of unemployed persons expressed as a percent of the labor force. The employment-
L544:     population ratio is the proportion of the civilian noninstitutional population 16 years
L545:     of age and older that is employed.
L546: 
L547:     Method of estimation. Estimates for 48 of the 50 states, the District of Columbia, the
L548:     Los Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale metropolitan division, New York City, and the balances
L549:     of California and New York State are produced using estimating equations based on
L550:     regression techniques. This method uses data from several sources, including the CPS,
L551:     the Current Employment Statistics (CES) survey of nonfarm payroll employment, and state
L552:     unemployment insurance (UI) programs. Estimates for the state of California are derived
L553:     by summing the estimates for the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale metropolitan division
L554:     and the balance of California. Similarly, estimates for New York State are derived by
L555:     summing the estimates for New York City and the balance of New York State. Estimates for
L556:     all nine census divisions are based on a similar regression approach that does not
L557:     incorporate CES or UI data. Estimates for census regions are obtained by summing the
L558:     model-based estimates for the component divisions and then calculating the unemployment
L559:     rate. Each month, census division estimates are controlled to national totals; state
L560:     estimates are then controlled to their respective division totals. A detailed
L561:     description of the estimation procedures is available from BLS upon request.
L562: 
L563:     Annual revisions. Labor force and unemployment data for prior years reflect adjustments
L564:     made at the beginning of each year. The adjusted estimates incorporate updated
L565:     population controls from the U.S. Census Bureau, any revisions in the other data sources,
L566:     and model re-estimation. Historical data for the most recent 5 years (both seasonally
L567:     adjusted and not seasonally adjusted) are revised near the beginning of each calendar
L568:     year, prior to the release of January estimates.
L569: 
L570:     The population controls for April 2020 forward reflect a "blended base," using population
L571:     totals from the 2020 Census but demographic distributions still based on the 2010 Census.
L572:     Typically, population estimates are revised back to the decennial estimates base. However,
L573:     due to notable discontinuities between the final, 2010-extrapolated recontrol series and
L574:     the new blended base series that coincide with the peak pandemic months of March�April
L575:     2020, BLS implemented a temporary wedged population series for the 2010s through March 2020.
L576:     For more information on these population controls, see
L577:     www.bls.gov/lau/important-information-on-revisions-to-data-for-model-based-areas-in-2022.htm.
L578: 
L579:     Reliability of the estimates
L580: 
L581:     The estimates presented in this release are based on sample surveys, administrative data,
L582:     and modeling and, thus, are subject to sampling and other types of errors. Sampling error
L583:     is a measure of sampling variability--that is, variation that occurs by chance because a
L584:     sample rather than the entire population is surveyed. Survey data also are subject to
L585:     nonsampling errors, such as those which can be introduced into the data collection and
L586:     processing operations. Estimates not directly derived from sample surveys are subject to
L587:     additional errors resulting from the specific estimation processes used. In table 1, level
L588:     estimates for states may not sum to level estimates for regions and divisions because of
L589:     rounding. Unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are computed from unrounded
L590:     levels and, thus, may differ slightly from rates and ratios computed using the rounded level
L591:     estimates displayed in table 1.
L592: 
L593:     Use of error measures. Changes in unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are
L594:     cited in the analysis of this release only if they have been determined to be statistically
L595:     significant. Furthermore, unemployment rates and employment-population ratios for the latest
L596:     year generally are cited only if they have been determined to be significantly different
L597:     from the corresponding U.S. measure. The underlying model-based error measures are available
L598:     online at www.bls.gov/lau/lastderr.htm. BLS uses 90-percent confidence levels in
L599:     determining whether changes in LAUS unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are
L600:     statistically significant. The average magnitude of the over-the-year change in an annual
L601:     state unemployment rate that is required in order to be statistically significant at the
L602:     90-percent confidence level is about 0.8 percentage point. The average magnitude of the
L603:     over-the-year change in an annual state employment-population ratio that is required in
L604:     order to be statistically significant at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.6
L605:     percentage point. Measures of nonsampling error are not available.
