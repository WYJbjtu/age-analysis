L542:     The labor force is the sum of employed and unemployed persons. The unemployment rate is the number
L543:     of unemployed persons expressed as a percent of the labor force. The employment-population ratio is
L544:     the proportion of the civilian noninstitutional population 16 years of age and older that is employed.
L545: 
L546:     Method of estimation. Estimates for 48 of the 50 states, the District of Columbia, the Los Angeles-Long
L547:     Beach-Glendale metropolitan division, New York City, and the balances of California and New York State
L548:     are produced using estimating equations based on regression techniques. This method uses data from
L549:     several sources, including the CPS, the Current Employment Statistics (CES) survey of nonfarm payroll
L550:     employment, and state unemployment insurance (UI) programs. Estimates for the state of California are
L551:     derived by summing the estimates for the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale metropolitan division and
L552:     the balance of California. Similarly, estimates for New York State are derived by summing the estimates
L553:     for New York City and the balance of New York State. Estimates for all nine census divisions are
L554:     based on a similar regression approach that does not incorporate CES or UI data. Estimates for census
L555:     regions are obtained by summing the model-based estimates for the component divisions and then
L556:     calculating the unemployment rate. Each month, census division estimates are controlled to national
L557:     totals; state estimates are then controlled to their respective division totals. Estimates for Puerto
L558:     Rico are derived from a monthly household survey similar to the CPS. A detailed description of the
L559:     estimation procedures is available from BLS upon request.
L560:     Annual revisions. Labor force and unemployment data for prior years reflect adjustments made at the
L561:     beginning of each year. The adjusted estimates incorporate updated population controls from the U.S.
L562:     Census Bureau, any revisions in the other data sources, and model re-estimation. The population
L563:     controls reflect a "blended base," with elements from three independent data sources for April 1, 2020.
L564:     In most years, historical data for the most recent 5 years (both seasonally adjusted and not seasonally
L565:     adjusted) are revised near the beginning of each calendar year, prior to the release of January
L566:     estimates. Though the labor force estimates typically are updated for 5 years, the population controls
L567:     are revised back to the most recent decennial estimates base (April 2020).
L568: 
L569:     Reliability of the estimates
L570:     The estimates presented in this release are based on sample surveys, administrative data, and modeling
L571:     and, thus, are subject to sampling and other types of errors. Sampling error is a measure of sampling
L572:     variability�that is, variation that occurs by chance because a sample rather than the entire population
L573:     is surveyed. Survey data also are subject to nonsampling errors, such as those which can be introduced
L574:     into the data collection and processing operations. Estimates not directly derived from sample surveys
L575:     are subject to additional errors resulting from the specific estimation processes used. In table 1,
L576:     level estimates for states may not sum to level estimates for regions and divisions because of rounding.
L577:     Unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are computed from unrounded levels and, thus,
L578:     may differ slightly from rates and ratios computed using the rounded level estimates displayed in table
L579:     1.
L580: 
L581:     Use of error measures. Changes in unemployment rates and employment-population ratios are cited in the
L582:     analysis of this release only if they have been determined to be statistically significant. Furthermore,
L583:     unemployment rates and employment-population ratios for the latest year generally are cited only if
L584:     they have been determined to be significantly different from the corresponding U.S. measure. The
L585:     underlying model-based error measures are available online at www.bls.gov/lau/lastderr.htm. BLS uses
L586:     90-percent confidence levels in determining whether changes in LAUS unemployment rates and employment-
L587:     population ratios are statistically significant. The average magnitude of the over-the-year change in
L588:     an annual state unemployment rate that is required in order to be statistically significant at the 90-
L589:     percent confidence level is about 0.5 percentage point. The average magnitude of the over-the-year
L590:     change in an annual state employment-population ratio that is required in order to be statistically
L591:     significant at the 90-percent confidence level is about 0.8 percentage point. Measures of nonsampling
L592:     error are not available.
