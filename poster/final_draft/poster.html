Demographic Change, Regional Structure, and Age-Selective Migration
in Japan and the United States

Shared population aging does not imply a single regional economic or demographic pattern.

REGIONAL EVIDENCE · JAPAN & THE UNITED STATES United States Japan2019 COMPARISON · 2022 FOCUS
1

Shared aging,
uneven regions

Both countries are aging. Japan has the higher 65+ share, while national averages conceal substantial regional variation.

2

Economic structure:
no single regional rule

The pooled aging-manufacturing association is positive. Within either country, the linear relationship is close to zero.

INTERPRETATION

99.1% of pooled covariance lies between country means. The pooled relationship should not be read as a common within-country regional pattern.

Covariance accounting, not explained variance or a causal contribution.
3

Age-selective migration:
demographic process

Young-adult net migration is strongly negatively associated with regional aging in Japan. The U.S. pattern is weaker and more uncertain.

ROBUSTNESS & UNCERTAINTY

Japan: r = -0.820; without Tokyo, -0.796 (2019: -0.812). U.S.: -0.362; without DC, -0.212 (2019: -0.337). 35/51 U.S. young-rate intervals include zero.

2022 total: +38,023. Ages 15-19: +13,795; ages 20-24: +57,153. All other ages combined: -32,925, including 65+: -9,284.

Key Findings

01Both Japan and the United States are aging, but regional aging patterns vary widely.

02The pooled aging-manufacturing association mainly reflects country differences, not a common within-country regional pattern.

03Age-specific flows reveal demographic process: the young-adult association is strong in Japan, but weaker and more uncertain in the U.S.

Limitations

Descriptive associations, not causal effects; one year's flow cannot explain an accumulated age structure.

Japan uses constructed rates from rounded population denominators. The 2019 official-rate table and exact age-reference day remain unverified.

ACS survey residence changes differ from Japanese calendar-year registrations; U.S. estimates carry sampling uncertainty.

Population dates and GDP periods differ (U.S. CY / Japan FY). Total migration can conceal offsetting age balances.
