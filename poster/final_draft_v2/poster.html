1 Shared aging,
uneven regions

Both countries aged over 2000-2022, with Japan at a higher level. Yet national trajectories conceal broad regional variation. The complete 2022 distribution establishes differences within each country; it does not make a state equivalent to a prefecture or identify the causes of aging.

National UN estimates and regional Census / Japan estimates use different methods. Regional medians are not population-weighted national rates.

2 Economic structure and aggregation

The pooled association is positive, but within-country linear associations are close to zero in both snapshots. Country differences dominate the pooled pattern. This does not establish that manufacturing is irrelevant, rule out nonlinear relationships, or show that all confounders have been controlled.

99.1% of 2022 pooled covariance corresponds to between-country group-mean differences.
Covariance accounting, not explained variance or a causal contribution.

3 Age-selective migration reveals opposing balances

Japan's young-adult association is strong and negative; the U.S. pattern is weaker and DC-sensitive. Age-selective balances add demographic detail, but a year's migration flow does not explain the accumulated age structure. Survey residence changes and registered migration events are distinct measures.

2019 r: Japan -0.812, USA -0.337. In 2022, without Tokyo: -0.796; without DC: -0.212.

Key Findings

01 Shared aging coexists with substantial regional variation.

02 The pooled aging-manufacturing relationship is not a shared within-country pattern.

03 Young-adult migration is strongly associated with aging in Japan; the U.S. pattern is weaker and more uncertain.

Limitations

Descriptive evidence, not causal effects. Japanese rates are constructed from rounded population denominators; the 2019 official-rate table and exact age-reference day remain unverified.

ACS sampling error, spatial dependence and shared denominators complicate interpretation. Population dates differ (U.S. July / Japan October); GDP uses CY / FY. Two snapshots do not establish every year's pattern.
