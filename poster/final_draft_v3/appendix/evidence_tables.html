Supplementary evidence

These are existing validated result tables. Equal-region weights; migration countries remain separate. The 65+ and complete year-comparison results are retained here so that the main poster can prioritize readable plots.

Older-adult coefficients are descriptive point summaries, not survey-uncertainty-adjusted country differences. Japan's 2022 65+ result is sensitive to Tokyo; the older-adult pattern is not stable across years. ACS ratio intervals use available MOEs and omit unavailable covariances, including numerator-denominator covariance. Estimates from the same survey are not known to be independent.

Japan's migration rates are constructed from official population estimates rounded to 1,000 persons. The 2019 denominator uses the original annual vintage, while the primary aging stock uses Member 1's revised vintage. The 2019 official-rate table and exact Japanese migration age-reference day remain unverified. ACS uses residence changes over the preceding year and age at interview; Japan uses calendar-year registration events, which can count repeated moves. These are not harmonized event measures.

U.S. private services include utilities but exclude government and government enterprises. Japan's published tertiary aggregate includes utilities, public administration, education and health/social services. The separate trends do not authorize a pooled service analysis, cross-country service gap, combined ranking or harmonized service definition. Manufacturing is nominal manufacturing value added divided by same-region, same-period nominal GDP, not employment share.

The covariance decomposition uses denominator N throughout and country weights 51/98 and 47/98. It is covariance accounting, not explained variance or causation. One year's migration flow cannot account for the accumulated stock of aging, and two snapshots do not establish all panel years.
