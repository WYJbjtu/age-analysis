BACKGROUND & DATA

Population aging is shared by Japan and the United States, but national averages can conceal distinct regional patterns. We examine how population age structure relates to industrial composition and age-selective domestic migration, distinguishing differences between countries from variation within them.

Data. We cover 51 U.S. state-level regions and 47 Japanese prefectures. National trends span 2000-2022; regional population and industry panels span 2011-2022. Association and migration comparisons use 2019 and 2022. Sources include UN WPP2024, U.S. Census/ACS, Japan's Statistics Bureau/e-Stat, BEA and Japan's Cabinet Office.

Both countries aged, with Japan at a higher level, while regional variation remains substantial. UN national estimates and regional official estimates use distinct methods; equal-region medians are not population-weighted national rates. These distributions describe administrative units, rather than causes of aging.

INDUSTRIAL STRUCTURE

Manufacturing medians moved in different directions over the panel. Services also changed within each country, under distinct definitions. For manufacturing, the positive pooled association is not reproduced as a strong linear relationship within either country under equal-region weighting.

2019 Pearson r: pooled 0.619; USA 0.040; Japan -0.010.

99.1% of 2022 pooled covariance corresponds to between-country mean differences.Covariance accounting, not explained variance or causation.

AGE-SELECTIVE MIGRATION

2019 Pearson r: Japan -0.812, USA -0.337. In 2022, without Tokyo: -0.796; without DC: -0.212.

In 2022, ages 15-19 (+13,795) and 20-24 (+57,153) outweighed all other ages (-32,925, already including 65+: -9,284). Total net migration conceals opposing age balances; one year's flows do not explain the accumulated age structure.

KEY FINDINGS

03Young-adult net migration is strongly negatively associated with regional aging in Japan; the U.S. pattern is weaker and more uncertain.
